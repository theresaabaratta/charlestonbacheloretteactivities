Charleston Bachelorette Activities

A bach weekend in Charleston runs on three things: time on the water, long meals, and a late night on King Street. Everything else is filler. This guide skips the filler and points you to the boats, tables, bars and local crews worth booking, with real prices so the group chat can settle the budget before anyone buys a flight.

Most groups land Thursday or Friday, stay downtown, and walk or rideshare everywhere. Plan one big daytime booking, one big dinner and one big night out per day, then leave space for porch drinks and wandering. That rhythm works for a crew of six or twenty.

Daytime Plans Between Brunch and Dinner

The afternoon is where a bach trip either drags or flies by. For something quiet and scenic, the Coastal Expeditions sunset paddle on Shem Creek puts the group in kayaks on the marsh while shrimp boats roll back into Mount Pleasant. Expect around $45–$65 a person, and book the last time slot of the day for the best light.

Groups that want a drink in hand can head to High Wire Distilling in the NoMo area, where the tour runs about $10 and the tasting room pours spirits made from Southern heirloom grains. Pair it with an hour at Charleston Escape Games on Upper King ($30–$40 each) for a little friendly competition before dinner.

For a slower pace, the Candlefish candle studio on King Street runs hands-on classes for about $60 a person, and everyone leaves with a custom scent that doubles as a party favor. From there, King Street itself is the afternoon: boutiques, shoe stores and coffee stops stacked block after block. Browsing is free; budget $50–$200 if the crew actually shops.

After dark, a ghost walk through the historic district is a cheap, fun way to see the old streets ($35–$50 each), or book a private horse-drawn carriage during the day for roughly $250–$325 per group.

Spring and fall weekends sell out early. Lock in tours and classes two to three months ahead.

Boat Days on the Harbor

Nothing gets booked faster in this city than a boat. No bar lines, your own playlist, and on most charters your own cooler. The right pick comes down to group size and budget.

For a dressed-up afternoon, Charleston Yacht Club runs private yacht outings from the downtown marina on the Ashley River, roughly $600–$1,000 an hour for up to 40 guests. Smaller crews can look at Charleston Yacht Tours for morning or full-day charters out of the harbor.

The Friki Tiki from Tiki Tours of Charleston is a 2.5-hour harbor cruise on a floating tiki bar, flat-rated at roughly $500–$700 for up to 20 people, with a sound system, a restroom and an optional splash stop. If the budget is tighter, Saltwater Cycle is the pedal-powered party boat out of the downtown marina. Seats run $50–$55 on public trips, pedaling is optional, and private runs start near $675.

Group size: Under 10 people, a shared cruise or pedal boat is the best value. Over 15, a private charter usually works out cheaper per head.

Timing: Afternoon departures beat morning ones. Nobody wants to board a boat at 9am after night one.

Lead time: Reserve four to six weeks out for any spring or fall weekend.

Rides, Drivers and Bar Crawls

Downtown parking is tight and expensive, so let someone else drive. Charleston Black Cab Co. books vintage London-style cabs with a private driver for a five-hour day of sightseeing, photo stops and rides between plans, quoted by group size. For the night, Pub Tour Charleston leads walking crawls through the old taverns downtown and keeps the group together between stops.

Short hops around the peninsula are easy on Lyft or Uber, usually $8–$20 a ride. Bigger crews that want to stay in one vehicle all night should price a party bus or limo at around $200–$400 an hour.

Brunch, Biscuits and Big Dinners

Brunch is a group sport here. Church & Union on North Market seats big parties under the vaulted ceilings and stained glass of a century-old church, and the mimosa flight belongs in front of the bride ($25–$45 with drinks). Poogan's Porch has been serving out of an 1891 Victorian in the French Quarter since 1976; ask for the upstairs porch and order shrimp and grits. Iron Rose at the Mills House does courtyard brunch with live weekend music, and Basic Kitchen is the lighter, plant-forward reset for day two.

For grab-and-go mornings, Callie's Hot Little Biscuit on Upper King makes scratch biscuits stuffed with pimento cheese, country ham or hot honey chicken. Order ahead in their app to skip the line. Sweet Palm Coffee covers house coffee, sweets and a breakfast-to-lunch menu where the chicken Caesar wrap is the order, and Welton's Tiny Bakeshop handles the Southern pastries.

Seafood lovers should put their names down at 167 Raw on King Street (no reservations) and wander until the table is ready; oysters, lobster rolls and tuna burgers run $40–$75 with drinks. The Ordinary turns an old bank into an oyster hall where the raw bar tower and a bottle of bubbles make the celebration dinner. For the dressed-up night, Oak Steakhouse on Broad Street is the classic call at $80–$150 a person.

Before dinner, string together a rooftop happy hour: Stars Rooftop for the 360-degree view, the rooftop at The Vendue, then The Watch for harbor views. Plan $40–$70 each across all three. Close the night at Vern's on Meeting Street, where craft cocktails upstairs give way to a dim speakeasy bar below.

Every pick here is our own. No restaurant pays to be included.

Where the Night Goes

Start somewhere pretty, end somewhere loud. Camellias, the pink champagne bar inside Hotel Bennett, is the photo stop. Ritual on Calhoun brings a Tulum-style rooftop with DJs and bottle service, while Republic Reign on King mixes a garden patio with VIP tables. Bourbon N Bubbles sits in between with a long bourbon list and champagne by the glass.

When it's time to dance, On Air stacks three floors on King Street, with honky-tonk downstairs and house music up top. The Commodore is all disco ball, and Trio keeps live bands on one floor and DJs on another. Babylon leans into salsa and bachata, and Prohibition brings 1920s swing and jazz on weekend nights.

For a sing-along, John King Grill runs dueling pianos Wednesday through Saturday, and The Bangkok Lounge does karaoke and frozen drinks every night of the week. Quieter options include the Thoroughbred Club at Charleston Place and Last Saint on the east side. And when someone needs a late burger and a pool table, A.C.'s Bar & Grill is the dive that never lets anyone down.

Every bar and club in this section is 21+. Bring ID for the whole group.

Decor, Setup and Planning Help

For the maid of honor who doesn't want to inflate balloons at midnight, a few local teams will style the rental before anyone arrives. Last Fling Charleston sells decor packages with mimosa bars and VIP add-ons, running about $865–$1,465 for a party of six. Southern Soirée is a Charleston-born planner known for custom sugar cookies and personal details, with packages from around $350. Bienvenue Charleston handles balloon garlands, photo backdrops and fridge stocking so the house is ready when the group walks in. Get a quote, but most setups land between $400 and $1,000.

Places That Keep the Group Together

The best home base is one where nobody needs a car. The Vendue is an art-filled boutique hotel two blocks from the water, and its rooftop makes an easy meeting spot ($250–$450+ a room per night). HarbourView Inn on East Bay puts the Market, Rainbow Row and King Street all within a ten-minute walk.

For groups of eight to twelve, a whole house beats a block of rooms. East Bay Company rents historic multi-bedroom rowhouses for about $700–$1,200+ a night, which splits nicely across a full crew.

Downtown fills up fast, especially during fall wedding season. Book lodging three or more months out.

A Four-Day Game Plan

A Thursday-to-Sunday trip gives the group enough time to do the big things without rushing any of them.

Thursday · Arrive

Check into the rowhouse with decor already set up, order in while the late flights land, and toast on the porch.

Friday · Warm Up

Brunch at Basic Kitchen, a distillery tour and a paint-and-sip in the afternoon, dinner at The Ordinary, then drinks at The Watch.

Saturday · Big Day

Sunrise paddle at Shem Creek, the food tour after lunch, an early dinner at Red's on the creek, then an escape room before the night out.

Sunday · Recover

Spa time at Woodhouse, one last brunch with photos on the porch, then pack up and head to the airport.

Leave one open block every day. The best moments of a bach trip usually aren't on the schedule.

When To Go

Spring: March sits around 70° by day and mid-40s at night. April warms into the mid-70s, and May feels like early summer in the low 80s.

Fall: September still runs mid-80s. October drops to the upper 70s, and November settles into crisp days near 70°.

Summer works too, but plan boats and walking tours for early or late in the day.
