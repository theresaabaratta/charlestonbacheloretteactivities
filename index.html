Charleston Bachelorette Activities

Planned by the clock, from biscuits to last call

Most Charleston guides hand you a list and leave you to sort it out. This one is built around the shape of an actual day: what to do when everyone's moving slow, where to be when the sun drops, and where the night ends up. Pick a lane for each block and the weekend plans itself.

It also leaves the peninsula. Some of the best hours of a bach trip happen on a boat in the harbor, on the sand at Folly or Isle of Palms, or on a Shem Creek deck in Mount Pleasant, so those are here too.

Mornings Worth Getting Up For

8am to noon

Nobody wants a full sit-down at 9am after night one, so start with a runner. Send one person up King Street to Callie's Hot Little Biscuit with an order placed in the app, and bring back a box of ham and hot honey chicken biscuits for the porch. Sweet Palm Coffee handles the coffee run and a breakfast-to-lunch menu for anyone who wants more than a biscuit.

The proper brunch belongs to the morning after the big night. Church & Union is the one built for a crowd, with long tables under stained glass in a converted church on North Market, and it books on OpenTable. High Cotton on East Bay does a dressier Lowcountry brunch with live jazz. Millers All Day on Lower King is the photogenic pick for hot honey chicken and grits, but weekend lines get long, so call ahead with a big group.

Booking the private show for brunch? Put it on the morning after the big night, while everyone is still in pajamas at the rental.

Afternoons on the Water and the Sand

Noon to 5pm

Out on the Harbor

A boat is the single most popular afternoon in town, and the format matters more than the boat. The Party Cat crew runs private charters with a three-hour island stop, plus a shared DJ cruise for groups too small to fill a boat. The Friki Tiki is a floating tiki bar that takes up to 20 for a flat group rate, with a splash stop if anyone wants to jump in. Book an afternoon departure. Morning boats after a late night rarely go well.

A Beach Day Instead

Two islands, two moods. Folly Beach is the laid-back surf town, where Isla Surf School runs group lessons and Charleston SUP Safaris paddles the flat Folly River. Isle of Palms is quieter, with the big county park on Front Beach and Barrier Island Eco Tours leaving the marina for Capers Island. Both are dry beaches, so the drinks stay on the porch.

Staying Downtown

For a group that would rather not leave the peninsula, take a guided paddle with Coastal Expeditions out of Shem Creek, or spend the afternoon working down King Street's boutiques before an early dinner.

Golden Hour Across the Bridge

5pm to 7pm

The best sunset in the area is a ten-minute ride over the Ravenel Bridge, on Shem Creek in Mount Pleasant. Red's Ice House is the old shrimp-boat icehouse turned deck bar, and the rooftop crow's nest looks straight down the creek as the boats come in. Order peel-and-eat shrimp and a bucket of beers. If the group wants to sit down, NICO next door has a wood-fired, French-leaning menu and the best oyster list on the creek.

On the beach islands, sunset is easy: walk drinks from Pier 101 out onto the Folly pier, or catch it from the deck at Islander 71 at the Isle of Palms marina.

Dinner for the Whole Crew

7pm to 9pm

Most famous Charleston dining rooms are built for four, so pick places that can hold you. For the dress-up night, Sorelle on Broad Street has a third-floor private room for ten, with full buyouts for bigger groups, and books 60 days out. Husk on Queen Street handles private events by email, so reach out the day the date is set.

On the casual nights, go loud. Lewis Barbecue in NoMo serves Texas brisket at the counter and has a side yard made for pitchers of margaritas. Leon's on Upper King does fried chicken and chargrilled oysters on a patio, and Xiao Bao Biscuit serves bold Asian plates in a converted gas station. Spending the night in Mount Pleasant? Page's Okra Grill takes reservations and has the shrimp and grits.

The full breakdown of who can seat ten or more, and which famous spots to skip with a big group, is in our restaurant guide.

Upper King After Dark

9pm to last call

Start the night dressed up. Camellias, the pink champagne bar at Hotel Bennett, is the group photo, and the rooftop at Ritual on Calhoun has DJs and tables with a skyline view.

Then pick your energy. For dancing, On Air has three floors on King with honky-tonk on the bottom and house music up top, and The Commodore is pure disco ball. For a sing-along, John King Grill runs dueling pianos late in the week, and The Bangkok Lounge does karaoke every night.

Everything in this section is 21+. Leave the cars parked and rideshare home. Our downtown parking guide covers where to leave them.

Home Base and the Setup

Where the weekend lives

Pick a base based on which half of the weekend matters more. A downtown rowhouse from East Bay Company sleeps eight to twelve within walking distance of King Street. For the beach version, Tides Folly Beach puts everyone in oceanfront rooms next to the pier, and Wild Dunes on Isle of Palms has villas and homes with resort perks.

To walk into a house that's already decorated, Southern Soirée handles setups and custom cookies, and Bienvenue Charleston does balloon garlands, backdrops and stocks the fridge before you land. Beach towns ban balloons on the sand, so keep the arch inside the rental.

A Friday-to-Sunday Plan

Three days, one boat, one beach, one big dinner. Swap pieces in from any section above.

Friday · Land and Loosen Up

Check in, then brisket and margaritas in the Lewis Barbecue side yard. Champagne at Camellias, then dancing at On Air.

Saturday · The Big One

A biscuit run to Callie's, then an afternoon Party Cat charter with the island stop. Sunset at Red's on Shem Creek, the private room at Sorelle, and The Commodore to close it out.

Sunday · Slow and Salty

A brunch-slot show at the rental, then Church & Union. One last afternoon on the sand at Folly before the airport run.

Leave a few hours open each day. The best stories from a bach trip usually happen between bookings.

Pick Your Vibe

Classy: Sorelle, Camellias, High Cotton jazz brunch

Rowdy: Party Cat, On Air, Lewis Barbecue

Beachy: Folly surf lesson, Pier 101, Red's at sunset

Low-key: Callie's on the porch, a Folly River paddle, karaoke
