Best Restaurants for a Charleston Bachelorette Party

Charleston has hundreds of restaurants, but only a small number can seat a group of ten or more without splitting you across the room. Most of the famous names are small, chef-driven spots built for tables of four: gorgeous for a date, brutal for a bride tribe of twelve.

The trick isn't finding the city's best restaurant. It's finding the ones built to hold your whole group, booking them in the right order, and saving the tiny famous places for when the crew splits up.

The One Big Reservation

Pick one dress-up dinner for the group photo and build the rest of the weekend around it. Both of these have private rooms, and both fill up early.

Sorelle

88 Broad Street
  • Southern Italian, Michael Mina group
  • Private room seats 10
  • 25 for a standing reception
  • Full buyout up to 60
  • Books 60 days out
Reserve Private Room: 10

Husk

76 Queen Street
  • Southern, all regional ingredients
  • Historic two-story house
  • Several private event spaces
  • Email the events team
  • Sidewalk lines at opening
Reserve Book Early

For a group of ten or fewer, Sorelle's third-floor private dining room is the best seat in town for a big night. It's on Broad Street across from St. Michael's, with handmade pasta and a horseshoe bar downstairs for the pre-dinner round. Bigger groups should ask about a full buyout. Husk runs out of a 19th-century house on Queen Street and handles private events by email. Reach out as soon as the date is set, because regular tables there are scarce.

The Loud, Easy Group Dinner

Not every night needs a reservation stress test. These places are built for noise, shared plates and a group that wants to eat and get to the bar.

Lewis Barbecue on North Nassau Street in NoMo serves Central Texas brisket, ribs and sausage cut to order at the counter. There are no reservations, so grab a pitcher of margaritas and claim a spot in the side yard, which is made for big, loud groups. Leon's Oyster Shop at 698 King is the fried chicken and chargrilled oyster stop, with a patio that suits a crowd. Friday nights fill up, so get there early or call ahead. Xiao Bao Biscuit sits in a converted 1940s gas station at Rutledge and Spring, serving bold Asian street food and strong cocktails in a casual room where jeans are fine.

Brunch That Can Actually Seat You

Brunch is a bachelorette rite of passage here, but plenty of the pretty spots top out at six people. These handle a crowd better.

Church & Union on North Market is the big-group brunch: long tables under the vaulted ceiling and stained glass of a converted church, and it books on OpenTable. High Cotton on East Bay does a classic Lowcountry brunch with live jazz and takes groups well with notice. Millers All Day at 120 King is the photogenic all-day breakfast spot, known for hot honey chicken and Geechie Boy grits. Weekend crowds are heavy, so call ahead before you show up with ten.

Poogan's Porch in its 1891 Victorian on Queen Street is better for eight or fewer who want the porch photo more than a rowdy morning. For a slow start at the rental, pick up coffee and pastries from Sweet Palm Coffee on King Street, or order ahead from Callie's Hot Little Biscuit on Upper King.

Famous Spots Better Saved for Small Groups

These show up on every best-of-Charleston list, and they deserve to, but each one fights a big-group reservation. Save them for when the crew splits into fours.

FIG on Meeting Street. Arguably the best food downtown, in a tight room built for small parties. Book well ahead, and call before trying for anything over eight.

Chubby Fish on Coming Street. About 40 seats, no reservations, and a line at opening. Perfect for four, painful for twelve.

167 Raw at 193 King. A small walk-in oyster bar with no reservations. Split up and put names on the list.

The Ordinary at 544 King. A stunning old bank turned oyster hall that works best as a cocktail-and-raw-bar stop for a few people, not a full sit-down for the whole group.

The Order to Book Everything In

First · The Big Saturday Dinner

Sorelle or Husk, depending on group size. Everything else gets scheduled around this one.

Second · Daytime Activities

Boat charters, kayak tours, tastings and spa slots fill four to six weeks out in peak spring and fall. See the main guide for the bookings worth locking in.

Last · Friday and Sunday Meals

These are the most flexible. The casual group spots above handle a medium-sized crew without much notice.

Every pick here is our own, and no restaurant pays to be listed. Group policies change, so confirm party size when you book.

Quick Picks

Big night: Sorelle, Husk

Easy dinner: Lewis Barbecue, Leon's, Xiao Bao Biscuit

Brunch: Church & Union, High Cotton, Millers All Day

Groups of four: FIG, Chubby Fish, 167 Raw
