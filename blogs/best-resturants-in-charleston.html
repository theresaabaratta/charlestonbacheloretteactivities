Best Restaurants For A Charleston Bachelorette Party (When Your Group Is 10+)

Charleston has hundreds of restaurants and a genuinely small number of them can actually seat a bachelorette-sized crowd without splitting you up. Here's the real breakdown — what to book, how far out, and what to skip.

Local Picks · 7 min read

Most of Charleston's most-hyped restaurants are small, chef-driven spots built for tables of four. Gorgeous for a date night, brutal for a bride tribe of twelve. The trick to a good bachelorette weekend isn't finding the "best" restaurant in the city — it's finding the ones that were actually built to seat your whole group at once, and booking them before someone else does. Below is how we'd sequence it.

The One Big Reservation

Pick a single "dress up and take the group photo" dinner and build the rest of the weekend around it. Anything worth booking in this category needs a real lead time.

Downtown Splurge

Husk

Rutledge Ave

Book ~90 days out

Charleston's flagship Southern kitchen. The upstairs dining room is the one to request — it's the best shot at keeping a party of 10-12 together at one table instead of split across two.

King Street Italian

Sorelle

Upper King

Book ~60 days out

Newer than Husk, and arguably the single best room in the city for a big bachelorette dinner right now — the second floor was practically designed for a party of 12-16.

Chef-Driven

FIG

Meeting Street

Book 3-4 months out

The food is arguably unmatched downtown, but the room is tight — this one's really built for 8 or fewer. Call ahead if you're hoping to squeeze in a 10-top.

The Loud, Easy Group Dinner

Not every night needs a reservation stress test. These places are built for noise, big tables, and a group that wants to eat fast and get to the bar.

BBQ

Lewis Barbecue

Upper King

Walk-ins under 12 usually fine

Backyard picnic tables that comfortably hold a party of 20. Brisket, ribs, frozen margaritas — nobody's dressing up for this one.

Oysters

Leon's Oyster Shop

Spring Street

Arrive by 6 PM on Fridays

Fried chicken, oysters, and frozé on a patio that seats 8-14 without a fuss. One of the easiest big-group bookings downtown.

Fish

Chubby Fish

Upper King

Communal tables

Popular place with communal tables — a great bar dinner for a group that wants to quick out and get classy food.

Brunch That Can Actually Seat You

Bottomless brunch is basically a bachelorette rite of passage in Charleston — but plenty of the pretty spots cap out at six people. These don't.

Bottomless

Millers All Day

Upper King

Book ~1 week out for Sunday

The default bachelorette brunch. Bottomless mimosas, biscuits, and a back room that holds a group of 12 without feeling cramped.

Jazz Brunch

High Cotton

East Bay

Book ~3 weeks out

Live jazz and a bottomless deal that's one of the best values in the city for a group of 10 or more.

Historic Porch

Poogan's Porch

Queen Street

Book ~2 weeks out

A genuinely historic Charleston porch — better suited to a group of 8 or fewer who want the photo more than a rowdy morning.

Walk-In Friendly, No Planning Required

For the meals you don't want to think about — lunch between activities, or a slow morning at the rental.

Seafood

167 Raw

Church Street

No reservations

Lobster rolls and oysters. Show up around 11:30 with a group of eight and you'll beat the lunch line.

Asian Street Food

Xiao Bao Biscuit

Spring Street

Easy walk-in, 6-10 people

A converted gas station with a cool patio — an easy, unfussy stop for a mid-sized group between activities.

Coffee & Pastry

Sweet Palm Coffee

King Street

Order ahead for a big group

Grab coffees and pastries to bring back to the rental for a slow start — cheaper and easier than waiting on a sit-down table. See our full Eat & Drink picks for more like this.

Famous, But Not Built For Your Group

These come up on every "best of Charleston" list — and every one of them will fight you on a big-group reservation.

Hominy Grill

Beloved, but tiny. A party of 10 is looking at a 2-hour wait on a Saturday.

Magnolias

Long a bachelorette staple, but the food quality has slipped compared to newer splurges like Sorelle.

The Ordinary

A stunning old bank vault, but the booking process punishes big groups. Better as a quick cocktail-and-oyster stop than a full sit-down dinner.

The Order To Book Everything In

1

Lock the big Saturday dinner first

Husk, Sorelle, or FIG — whichever fits your group size. Everything else gets scheduled around this one.

2

Book your daytime activities next

Kayak tours, distillery tastings, and spa slots fill up 4-6 weeks out in peak spring and fall season.

3

Save Friday and Sunday meals for last

These are the most flexible — most of the walk-in-friendly spots above will take a medium-sized group same-day.
