Downtown Charleston Parking

The peninsula is small, the streets are narrow, and spaces go fast. You have four choices: city and county garages, street meters, metered lots, and residential streets with time limits. Pick by how long you're staying, read every sign, and when the night involves drinks, leave the car.

We're a bachelorette planning site, but this guide works for anyone parking downtown.

Where to Park, by How Long You're Staying

Under two hours: a street meter. They run $3 an hour with a one-hour minimum, Monday to Saturday from 9am to 6pm.

Two to eight hours: a garage. City garages charge $1 per 20 minutes with a $24 daily max and no time limit.

Evenings: a $5 flat rate after 5pm at select garages, and free meters after 6pm.

Drinking or staying overnight: take a rideshare or use hotel parking. Don't drive after drinking.

Before you pull in, check the day and the time, since Sundays, city holidays and anything after 6pm mean free meters. Read every posted sign, because meters and residential blocks often carry extra restrictions. Pay by card, text or app. If you use a garage, hang on to the ticket, because a lost one costs $50.

City and County Garages

City garages charge $1 per 20 minutes with a $24 daily max. As of October 2025, county garages charge the same. The daily max resets at midnight, so an overnight stay can be billed twice. Private garages and lots set their own prices, which usually run higher.

Best Garage for Each Part of Downtown

Upper King Bars and Restaurants

Use Midtown at 558 King, Marion Square at 399 King, or Camden/Exchange at 35 John. Street parking on King between Spring and John is banned from 6pm to 3am, Thursday through Saturday.

Lower and Middle King Shopping

Use Majestic at 211 King, which drops to $5 after 5pm, or Charleston Place at 85 Hasell. The county's King and Queen garage at 85 Queen also covers this stretch.

City Market and East Bay Dining

Street spots here go first, so head straight for Concord/Cumberland at 1 Cumberland, the county's Cumberland garage at 90 Cumberland, or Charleston Place.

Waterfront Park and the French Quarter

Use East Bay/Prioleau at 25 Prioleau or 93 Queen. Both have evening flat rates.

SC Aquarium, Harbor Boats and the Gaillard

The Aquarium garage at 24 Calhoun takes cash and card and sits right by the harbor tour docks. For a Gaillard Center show, the Gaillard garage at 32 Alexander and the Auditorium metered lots have EV plug-ins, and both fill early on show nights.

Meter Prices and Free Hours

Street meters run $3 an hour with a one-hour minimum. They're enforced Monday through Saturday, 9am to 6pm, and free on Sundays, city holidays and after 6pm. Pay by card, by text, through the app or with a city SmartCard. Check the posts for extra restrictions, and skip bagged meters, which are reserved.

The city's metered lots follow the same rules: $3 an hour, a one-hour minimum, 9am to 6pm, and free on Sundays and holidays.

How Residential Parking Works

Many downtown blocks are residential parking districts. Residents with a permit can park with no time limit, and everyone else gets the posted limit.

Visitors and the 2-Hour Signs

The rule: without a permit, stay only as long as the sign allows during the posted days and hours. That's usually two hours, though some blocks allow one or four.

Enforcement: permits are digital and plates are scanned, so don't count on a grace period.

Staying longer: get a one-day visitor pass from the resident you're visiting, or move to a garage.

Ticket traps: facing the wrong direction, yellow curbs, parking outside the painted lines and street-sweeping signs.

Apply online at parkcharleston-sc.com/permit, which activates on approval with a $2.50 card fee per item. You can also mail the application, documents and a check or money order to Revenue Collections, 180 Lockwood Blvd., Suite 234, Charleston, SC 29403, or apply in person by appointment.

Unpaid tickets have to be cleared before a permit is issued. Visitor booklets are mail or in-person only, paid by cash or check. A car that hasn't moved in seven days can be treated as abandoned, even with a permit. Questions go to 843-724-7375.

Monthly Parking Downtown

Downtown workers and residents without a driveway often rent by the month. The city currently has no monthly openings and no waiting list. The county's two garages offer 24/7 monthly access.

At the top rate, that adds up to $2,580 a year. Private lots, businesses and homeowners also rent spaces monthly, often through parking-sharing apps, and prices vary.

One or two people: drive. For a daytime trip, a garage costs less than a round-trip Uber.

Four or more: it's close. A Mount Pleasant round trip split four ways is about $9 each, and UberXL seats six.

Drinking: always Uber. It's cheaper than a ticket, a tow or worse.

Tips for Visitors and Off-Peninsula Locals

Park once and ride CARTA's free DASH shuttle around downtown. Arrive after 5pm to get the $5 evening rate at Majestic, East Bay/Prioleau or 93 Queen, since the meters go free at 6. Sunday is the easiest day, when meters and metered lots cost nothing. Ask your hotel about overnight parking before you book. Driving something tall? Check the city's garage height clearances first. And pay any ticket on time to avoid late fees.
